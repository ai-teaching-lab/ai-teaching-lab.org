The Lab's judiciary workstream teaches the federal judiciary to work effectively with AI tools. Per the Lab's pillar architecture, this work sits in **Teach** — judicial AI training is teaching, not partnership-mediated Building work.

## What we're doing

- **Use-case research.** What are chambers actually doing with AI today? What works, what breaks, what needs to be more carefully governed?
- **Chambers-facing tools.** Practical software that helps chambers use AI well — without the false-confidence pitfalls.
- **AI use audits.** Frameworks chambers can use to assess their own current AI use.
- **Training and onboarding.** Material judges and clerks can use to come up to speed on AI capabilities and limitations.

## Why it matters

The judiciary is one of the highest-stakes settings for AI in legal work. Mistakes propagate through the legal system; over-reliance on AI tools without understanding their limits creates real risk for litigants. Teaching the bench to use AI carefully — to know when AI helps and when it doesn't — is some of the most consequential teaching the Lab does.

## Sensitivity

The most external-facing of the Lab's workstreams. Chambers context is presumptively confidential.
